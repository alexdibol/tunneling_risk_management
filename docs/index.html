Risk Geometry · Tunneling · Institutional Governance

Seeing Risk as a Dynamic Geometry

A research and teaching project that moves beyond static probabilities to examine how systems travel through state spaces, encounter protective barriers, activate hidden pathways, and approach adverse outcomes.

Tunneling and Risk Management combines classical stochastic dynamics, geometric modeling, network science, machine learning, and a disciplined quantum-enhanced representation of amplitude, phase, interference, action, and transmission. The project includes a complete 15-chapter book, companion Colab notebooks, empirical implementation blueprints, and governance frameworks for high-stakes institutional use.

The Research Question

How does risk become accessible?

The deepest risk question is not only the probability of failure. It is how the system is positioned, what protects it, which routes connect it to danger, how those routes interact, and whether intervention can alter the geometry in time.

Locate the system.
Map its state, manifold, metric, mobility, and distance from fragile or adverse regions.
Measure protection.
Estimate barrier height, width, continuity, reliability, capacity, and independence.
Govern accessibility.
Detect pathways, stress the geometry, and intervene before the transition becomes irreversible.
The Central Proposition

Risk is movement through a structured landscape

Borrowers, institutions, markets, operational systems, contracts, and networks occupy positions in multidimensional state spaces. Their trajectories are shaped by drift, covariance, jumps, constraints, protective barriers, pathway structure, and timing. Two systems with similar headline probabilities may therefore face radically different routes to failure—and require radically different interventions.

“Understand how risk can travel, measure how accessible the route has become, and act before the transition becomes irreversible.”

Two Complementary Perspectives

Classical first. Quantum-enhanced second.

The project does not replace classical risk modeling. It begins with conventional mechanisms and introduces the quantum-enhanced layer only where pathway interaction and timing provide stable empirical value.

Classical Risk Geometry

Movement, diffusion, thresholds, and contagion

The classical layer explains how systems move through drift, covariance, jumps, regime change, threshold crossing, barrier erosion, liquidity spirals, contagion, and network cascades.

  • Stochastic differential equations and state-space models
  • Covariance ellipsoids and mobility directions
  • Thresholds, hazards, survival models, and transition probabilities
  • Graph contagion, fire sales, runs, and common shocks
  • Classical benchmarks for every empirical application

Application Domains

One architecture, different institutional realities

The variables and pathways change across domains, but the structural questions remain the same: where is the system, what protects it, how can risk travel, and what intervention changes the route?

Domain 1

Credit Risk

Corporate, consumer, and sovereign risk through liquidity, refinancing, collateral, covenant, employment, reserve, confidence, and political pathways.

Dominant question: which route reaches default or restructuring with the least action?
Domain 2

Operational & Cyber Risk

Layered controls, human error, attack paths, infrastructure concentration, shared vendors, detection latency, containment, and recovery.

Dominant question: how many controls are truly independent under stress?
Domain 3

Legal, Regulatory & Conduct Risk

Contractual ambiguity, evidence quality, disclosure, conduct consistency, precedent, enforcement, remediation, and regulatory response.

Dominant question: is formal legal protection operationally enforceable?
Domain 4

Institutional & Systemic Risk

Networks, common assets, funding concentration, collateral systems, market infrastructures, policy capacity, and synchronized behaviour.

Dominant question: can individually safe institutions form a fragile system?
